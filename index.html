04

About

I study the political economy of information and institutions, with a broader interest in how states acquire capacity and how citizens respond to public authority.

My projects range from contemporary survey experiments to historical administrative records and conflict-zone geospatial data. Across these settings, I am interested in the same core problem: how formal rules and informal identities jointly determine whose preferences become visible, whose claims are heard, and where the state acts.
